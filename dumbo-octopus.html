Dumbo Octopus: The Deep-Sea Superhero

Dumbo floats 1 to 7 kilometres under the sea, where the water pressure could smash a glass jar — and even crush a metal ship! So how does its soft body survive down there? Grab your imaginary submarine and let’s look inside! 🔍

🧠 The big question

The Deep Sea Tries to SQUISH Everything

Would you believe a soft jelly creature is the one laughing at the pressure?

🛳

Penguin plop! 🐱

People and most sea animals carry air pockets inside — in lungs, helmets or shells. Deep down, the heavy water squashes those air pockets like a giant fist. That’s why submarines need super-thick metal walls!

🐙

But Dumbo is a water balloon! 🌉

Dumbo’s body is soft, squishy and full of water — no air pockets, no hard shell. Try squeezing a water balloon: the water slips away and nothing breaks. Pressure just washes right through Dumbo’s jelly body — so the deep sea can’t crush it!

Down at 7,000 m the squeeze is about 700 times stronger than at the surface — like standing under lots of cars on every fingertip!

🔍 Look inside

Dumbo’s Deep-Sea Anatomy

Tap the numbered dots on the picture (or the cards) to learn what each body part does!

1 Finny Ears 👂

Most octopuses zoom by squirting jets of water. Dumbo is different: it flaps these two fins like soft little wings to swim, glide and hover.

⚡ Energy-saver motor: flapping uses much less energy than jetting — super handy in a place where food is rare!

2 Night-Vision Eyes 👁

Dumbo’s home is almost pitch-dark. Its big, round eyes are amazing light-collectors, like the best night-camera in the world, so it can spot tiny snacks and stay out of trouble.

⚡ Sees in the blue-black dark: big eyes scoop up every tiny bit of dim light!

3 Squishy Jelly Mantle 🎈

The main body (called the mantle) is soft, jelly-like and full of water — a bit like a water balloon! No air pockets and no hard shell, so the crushing pressure just washes through.

⚡ Pressure-proof squish: the water inside pushes back evenly, so nothing gets crushed!

4 Umbrella Arms & Web ☂

Dumbo’s eight arms are joined by stretchy skin, like the fabric of an umbrella. It opens this “bell” to float slowly, turn gently and scoop up dinner.

⚡ A built-in sail: the web catches water like a parachute, helping Dumbo glide without extra work!

5 Fuzzy Cirri & Suckers 🧺

Along each arm are little finger-like fuzzies called cirri (say “seer-ee”) plus rows of tiny suckers. Cirri sweep tiny animals out of the soft mud, and the suckers hold on tight.

⚡ Vacuum + grabber: built-in tools for finding worms, tiny shrimp and snails in the dark!

6 Tiny Funnel — Gentle Jet 💨

Near its arms is a little tube called a funnel. Dumbo can give a slow puff of water for a small boost — like riding a scooter, not a rocket!

⚡ Slow and steady wins: tiny jets plus flappy fins mean almost all energy is saved for growing and exploring!

⭐ Bonus round

Dumbo’s Deep-Sea Superpowers

Four more body tricks that make Dumbo a deep-sea champion!

🚫

No Ink Tank

Most octopuses squirt an ink cloud to escape. Finned octopuses like Dumbo skip the ink — in the pitch-dark, a smoke cloud wouldn’t scare anyone, and making ink would waste precious energy!

🐢

Slow & Steady

Dumbo has a very low metabolism, which means its body uses only a little fuel each day. Perfect for a place where meals can be few and far between!

📏

Small & Light

Dumbo stays nicely small — about 20–30 cm, like a school ruler. A tiny body needs less food and can squeeze into snug, safe hiding spots.

❄️

Cold-Proof Jelly

Its jelly body works perfectly in near-freezing water. Dumbo is 100% built for the cold, dark abyss — and some visit warm spots near deep-sea vents!
