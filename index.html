✨ SUPER NEW! ✨ 🐍 “Nina” · the new species

Meet Nina the Northern Green Anaconda 🐍

In February 2024, scientists studying anacondas in the Amazon River made a big discovery — the northern green anacondas are a brand-new species! They look almost the same on the outside, but a secret science tool called DNA shows they are different. It’s like finding out a whole new family was hiding right under our noses!

  • 📏 Up to 26 ft long That’s as long as 4 kids lying head-to-toe!
  • 🌊 King of the river Loves the Amazon like a giant swimming pool!
  • 💪 Super-squeeze hug It’s a constrictor — no poison, just a strong hug!

🧩 Wow Facts

  • DNA magic: the new species looks almost the same, but its DNA is different — that’s how scientists cracked the case!
  • The heaviest: green anacondas are among the heaviest snakes on the planet.
  • Teamwork: scientists worked together with local Waorani people to make this amazing discovery possible!

Fun fact: Nina has NO venom — she’s a super-squeezer! But many snakes DO make venom… and humans turn it into medicine!

📰 New sighting · March 2025

Dumbo Octopus 🐙

The deepest-living octopus floats like a soft balloon in the dark sea.

🐙

Surprise!

Named after Dumbo the elephant because of its two ear-like fins! It is the deepest-living octopus known — up to 7 km down. The newest sighting was in the Gulf of Guinea in March 2025.

📰 Healing science

Axolotl 🦎

The “Mexican walking fish” — though it’s not a fish at all!

Tap to flip

🦎

Surprise!

This pink little cutie can regrow its legs, tail and gills — even parts of its heart and brain! Wild ones live in only ONE lake system in Mexico, so they are critically endangered. Scientists study them to help people heal too!

📰 New species still found each year

Peacock Spider 🕷

A tiny dancer that flashes a rainbow fan — smaller than a grain of rice!

Tap to flip

🕷

Surprise!

Only the males are rainbow-coloured — they dance and raise their colourful flaps to impress females! They are only about 4–5 mm long, and more than 100 species are known. Scientists keep finding new dancing spiders in Australia every year.
